Lettrd

Van Gogh's letters, one at a time.

Lettrd is a free iPhone app that delivers Vincent van Gogh's 907 letters one at a time, in English or Korean, at the pace he actually wrote them. A letter arrives, you read it, and then you wait — the way Theo did, not knowing what comes next.

Questions

Is there an app to read Vincent van Gogh's letters?

Lettrd is a free iPhone app built for exactly this. It holds all 907 of Van Gogh's surviving letters and delivers them one at a time rather than as a list you scroll, with the paintings and sketches he describes shown beside the text.

Can I read Van Gogh's letters in Korean?

Yes. Lettrd reads in English or Korean, and follows your iPhone's language. The translations were made from the public-domain Dutch and French originals rather than taken from a copyrighted scholarly edition.

Why does Lettrd only give me one letter at a time?

Because the waiting is the point. Van Gogh's correspondence ran for eighteen years, and reading it as one long archive flattens it. In Lettrd a letter arrives, you read it, and then you wait — for a few hours or a few days, in proportion to the silence Van Gogh himself left before writing again. You find out what happened next at roughly the speed Theo did.

Can I see the paintings Van Gogh describes in his letters?

Yes. When a letter mentions a work, the painting or the sketch he enclosed is shown next to the passage, so you can look at what he was describing as you read it. There is also a timeline of his life by period, from the Netherlands through Paris and Arles to Auvers.

Is Lettrd free? Does it have ads or in-app purchases?

Lettrd is free on the App Store, and the app contains no advertising and no in-app purchases. It needs an iPhone running iOS 18 or later.

Does Lettrd need an account, and what happens to my notes?

There is no account and no sign-up. The sentences you underline, the notes you write on them and your reading progress are kept on your iPhone and synced between your own devices through your private iCloud database — they are not sent to Lettrd's servers. The app also sends anonymous usage events, such as your country and which letter was opened, with no account or device identifier attached.
